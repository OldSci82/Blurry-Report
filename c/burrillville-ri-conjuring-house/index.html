Conjuring House lawsuits

HauntBurrillville, Rhode Island · Posted Nov 16, 2026

A family of seven said a Rhode Island farmhouse tormented them for nearly ten years, and the woman they blamed may have done nothing wrong at all.
Burrillville, Rhode Island. The farmhouse on Round Top Road in Harrisville, 1971 to 1980, and still in court in 2026.

In January 1971 Roger and Carolyn Perron moved their five daughters into an 18th century farmhouse on the edge of town. Over the next decade, the family said, they lived with apparitions, footsteps, shaking beds and unseen hands. The ghost Carolyn feared most was a woman with a broken neck. Ed and Lorraine Warren came to investigate, the family left in 1980, and in 2013 the story became The Conjuring, the start of one of the biggest horror franchises ever made. Later owners have reported doors moving on their own, knocking, voices and a drifting black mist.

The debate: the spirit was named Bathsheba Sherman, a supposed witch who sacrificed a baby. Bathsheba was a real woman who died in 1885, but researchers working through census records, her obituary and her will found no witchcraft, no murder and no link to the house. She was born a Thayer, married onto the Sherman farm, and her funeral was led by a Baptist pastor. Her gravestone has been vandalized over the years. The suicides and murders pinned on the farmhouse turned out to have happened miles away, to unrelated families who shared a surname. Norma Sutcliffe, who owned it from 1987 to 2019, ran a daycare inside for twenty years.

Now the house is haunted by lawyers. After owner Jacqueline Nuñez defaulted on her mortgage, it landed in a tangle of lawsuits involving a bank, a YouTuber who bought the loan and Ghost Hunters star Jason Hawes, who says he bought the house. In September a Rhode Island judge called the cases "a mess" and said, "Something does not smell right."
