It Came Head On

CaseFargo, North Dakota · Posted Nov 19, 2026

A ball of light with no plane behind it turned and came head on at a fighter pilot over Fargo, and four other people watched it happen.
Fargo, North Dakota. Hector Airport, about 9 PM, October 1, 1948.

Lt. George F. Gorman of the North Dakota Air National Guard stayed up in his F-51 Mustang to log some night flying after the rest of his squadron landed. The tower cleared him in and warned him about one Piper Cub nearby. Then a blinking light passed him on the right, and the tower said there was no other traffic. He went after it. The light was six to eight inches across, perfectly round with sharp edges, and when he closed in it stopped blinking and broke into a hard left bank.

For 27 minutes it out turned and out climbed him. Twice it headed straight at him, and the first time he dove to keep from colliding while it passed over his canopy. Controllers Lloyd Jensen and H. E. Johnson watched the light from the tower through binoculars, and Dr. A. E. Cannon and his passenger Einar Nielson saw it from the Piper Cub. At about 14,000 feet Gorman's Mustang stalled while the light kept rising, until it went straight up and vanished. He told investigators he was sure there was thought behind its maneuvers.

The debate: Project Sign investigators were there within hours and even ran a Geiger counter over his plane. At first they ruled out aircraft and balloons. Then the weather bureau said it had released a lighted balloon from Fargo at 8:50 PM, and the case was closed as a balloon, with the planet Jupiter blamed for the end of the chase. A balloon drifts with the wind. This light kept coming back at him.

Can a weather balloon play chicken with a Mustang?
