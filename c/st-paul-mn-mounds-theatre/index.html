Mounds Theatre ghosts (Red, Mary and Jim)

CaseSt. Paul, Minnesota · Posted Oct 19, 2026

St. Paul, Minnesota. October 2026.

The Mounds Theatre opened in 1922 in the Dayton's Bluff neighborhood on St. Paul's East Side, billed as "The Pride of Dayton's Bluff," with silent movies and live entertainment. Its first talkie played in 1929. By the 1960s the roof leaked on moviegoers, and in July 1967 the doors closed. The building sat for decades, used for storage, until it was donated to a youth nonprofit and restored. When the renovation began in 2001, workers found candy wrappers and popcorn bags still left from the last shows of 1967. It reopened in October 2003 with The Wizard of Oz.

That is when the stories started. Volunteers working on the building began reporting spirits, and they gave them names. Red is described as a cranky old man, and he is usually tied to the projection booth. Mary is a playful little girl who pushes a ball around, sometimes described in a pink dress on the stage. Jim is said to be an usher. People report cold spots, flickering lights, objects that disappear and a tap on the shoulder when nobody is there.

At the start of October, the official start of Halloween season, KSTP checked in on the theatre. "There are people who say they have gotten grabbed, scratched, touched; they have gotten footage of these people," said Heather Rand-McKay. "They have seen full-body apparitions. The lore is that three ghosts inhabit the theater." The theatre calls itself Minnesota's second most paranormally active location and runs tours in near-total darkness, climbing 19 stairs up to the old projection booth.

Is it really haunted? Even Rand-McKay says the crowds range "from skeptics to true believers," and some of the wilder stories told about the basement have never been independently verified. The theatre's own website puts it plainly: paranormal experiences are not guaranteed.
