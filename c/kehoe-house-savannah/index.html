The Children Upstairs, Kehoe House

HauntSavannah, GA · Posted Nov 18, 2026

Guests say small hands brush their faces in the dark at Savannah's Kehoe House, but the famous story of how those children died has no record behind it.
Savannah, Georgia. The Kehoe House on Columbia Square, built in 1892, now an inn.

William Kehoe came from Ireland as a boy, worked his way up from apprentice to owner of an iron foundry and built this towering Renaissance Revival mansion for his wife Anne and their ten children, trimmed everywhere in his own cast iron. After the family sold it around 1930, it became a boarding house and then, for decades, a funeral parlor. Football star Joe Namath owned it for a while before it became an inn.

Guests in two second floor rooms, including room 201, report being woken by what feels like the touch of small children. Others hear running feet and laughter in the halls late at night, even when no children are staying. The tour version explains it all: two Kehoe twins climbed into a chimney during hide and seek, got stuck and died, and their mother found them days later.

The debate: a Savannah Morning News search of birth, death and census records found no Kehoe twins and no chimney on any death certificate, and nine of the ten children survived infancy. A Kehoe descendant told one writer that two little girls did die young in the house, of illness, days apart. Ghost tour companies now call the chimney story debunked, and still the reports keep coming.

Would you sleep in room 201?
