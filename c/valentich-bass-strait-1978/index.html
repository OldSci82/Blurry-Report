Frederick Valentich disappearance (Oct 21, 1978)

CaseBass Strait, Australia · Posted Nov 26, 2026

"It's not an aircraft." Those were the last words a 20 year old pilot radioed before he vanished over the sea.
Melbourne, Australia. A Cessna 182 bound for King Island, over Bass Strait near Cape Otway, about 7:06 PM, October 21, 1978.

Frederick Valentich had about 150 hours of flying time when he called Melbourne Flight Service to ask about a large aircraft following him at 4,500 feet. There was no known traffic. He said it had four bright lights, passed about 1,000 feet overhead at high speed, and seemed to be "orbiting" above him with a shiny metal surface and a green light. Then he reported his engine was running rough.

At 7:12 PM he said, "It's not an aircraft." His microphone stayed open for 17 seconds of a metallic scraping noise, and then nothing. A sea and air search covering more than 1,000 square miles found no trace. Five years later an engine cowl flap from the same model of Cessna washed up on Flinders Island.

The debate: Department of Transport officials suggested he became disoriented and saw his own lights reflected in the water while flying upside down. Others think he staged his own disappearance. Skeptics point to a meteor stream that night, while ufologists point to people who said they saw an erratic green light in the sky.

What do you think was above him?
