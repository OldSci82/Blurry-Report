Old Charleston Jail (Lavinia Fisher legend vs. record)

HauntCharleston, South Carolina · Posted Nov 10, 2026

Charleston, South Carolina. The Old City Jail, 21 Magazine Street.

From 1802 to 1939 this was the Charleston County Jail. It held pirates waiting to hang, Union prisoners and soldiers of the 54th Massachusetts captured at Fort Wagner, and, in 1819 and 1820, John and Lavinia Fisher, in a six by eight cell. The 1886 earthquake took off its tower and top floor.

The legend says the Fishers ran the Six Mile Wayfarer House outside the city, where lone travelers got poisoned tea and a bed that dropped into a pit. Lavinia is often called America's first female serial killer. She supposedly wore her wedding dress to the gallows and told the crowd, "If you have a message you want to send to hell, give it to me, I'll carry it."

The controversy is that the record does not match the legend. The Fishers were convicted of highway robbery, a capital crime then, not murder, and historians now question whether Lavinia ever killed anyone at all.

The ghost stories stuck anyway. People working on the building during restoration in the 2000s reported apparitions, odd sounds, overpowering smells, and footprints in the dust of a building that was supposed to be empty. Ghost tours have run there since 2003. Since a roughly $15 million renovation it has been an event venue and office space, and in 2026 Garden & Gun moved its headquarters inside.

A murderess who never stopped talking, or a robber turned into a monster by two centuries of storytelling?
