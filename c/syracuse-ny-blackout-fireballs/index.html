Syracuse NY 1965 blackout fireballs over power lines; flight instructor Weldon Ross and deputy aviation commissioner Robert Walsh (November 9, 1965)

CaseSyracuse, New York · Posted Nov 16, 2026

The night 30 million people lost power, pilots over Syracuse watched balls of fire rise from the power lines.
Syracuse, New York. Hancock Field and the Clay to Niagara Falls high line, about 5:16 PM, November 9, 1965.

At 5:16 PM a relay tripped at the Sir Adam Beck power station near Niagara Falls, Ontario, and within minutes the grid collapsed across New York, parts of six neighboring states and eastern Canada. Some 30 million people sat in the dark. Sensors even put the government's Mount Weather bunker, built to shelter the president in a nuclear attack, on red alert.

Weldon Ross, a flight instructor with the Syracuse Flying School for 15 years, was in the air with his student James Brooking near Three Rivers. "We were over the high line... which runs from Clay, New York to Niagara Falls, New York when we saw the fire flash," Ross said. It was "a ball of orange-reddish fire which flared up bigger than a house fire," like a barn full of hay, and it lasted about 10 seconds. "I know I was over the power lines."

Robert Walsh, Syracuse's deputy city aviation commissioner, had also been flying when the lights went out. After he landed, he was sitting near the runway with others when "we saw a sudden ball of fire south of us. It appeared to be about 100 feet in the air and 50 feet in diameter." About ten minutes later they saw a second one. In Camillus, a mother and three of her children said a dome shaped fireball rose over the moon, and as it moved forward their lights dimmed. It pulled back and vanished in a flash just as the power died.

The debate: the official cause was a single protective relay at the Beck plant, and the Syracuse fireballs were blamed on dump fires, burning barns and arcing equipment. Power company spokesmen first denied any strange light over the Beck plant that night, then admitted hundreds of people had reported one. In 1968 atmospheric physicist James McDonald told a House committee that even the famous New York blackout involved UFO sightings.

One bad relay, or something hanging over the power lines?
