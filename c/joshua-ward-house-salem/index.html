Joshua Ward House, Salem

HauntSalem, MA · Posted Nov 17, 2026

Salem's most haunted house stands on the stones of the sheriff who carted the condemned to the gallows, and the photo that made it famous may show the wrong woman.
Salem, Massachusetts. The Joshua Ward House at 148 Washington Street, built in 1784, now The Merchant hotel.

In 1692 George Corwin, the 25 year old High Sheriff of Essex County, drove the condemned by cart to Proctor's Ledge, seized their belongings and oversaw the pressing to death of 73 year old Giles Corey. Legend says he pushed the dying man's tongue back into his mouth with his cane. Corwin's own house stood on this spot, and ragged stones from it still sit in the foundation. When he died in 1696, the story goes, the accused merchant Philip English threatened to seize his corpse over the goods Corwin took, so the family buried him in the cellar.

In 1784 merchant Joshua Ward built the brick mansion on the site, and George Washington slept there in 1789. Two centuries later it was a realty office, and staff reported cold breezes, fire alarms going off, candles found bent into curves and a man grabbed from behind on the stairs. Then, around Christmas, someone took Polaroid portraits of the staff for the office wreath. The print of a young employee by the front door came out showing someone else entirely, a woman with wild black hair.

The debate: the Salem Witch Museum says Corwin was actually buried in the Broad Street Cemetery, and historian Marilynne Roach suggests the cellar story grew from an arrest warrant saying a constable had "seized the body" of Corwin while he was still alive. No ghost report from the house has turned up before a ghost booklet from 1983 or 1984, nobody independent ever examined the print, and the author who published it later changed who was standing in front of the camera. The hotel says it has never seen or felt her.

Would you book a night in the house built on the sheriff's cellar?
