Multiple pulsing lights over tree line (wedding at The Factory at Potato Chip)

CaseCalifornia, Missouri · Posted Oct 19, 2026

California, Missouri. Around 9:15 p.m. on October 17, 2026.

A family is leaving a wedding at The Factory at Potato Chip, an event venue in California, Missouri, when the witness's mother spots bright lights low over the tree line to the northwest. The sky is clear and full of stars, and out here in the country there is almost no light pollution, so they stop to watch.

What they describe is a group of lights that pulse and change color, brighten and dim, and at times shine brighter than a helicopter's lights. New lights seem to join from above or rise out of the tree line, while others go completely dark and vanish without ever dropping below the trees. At one point the witness counts seven of them, circling each other and changing direction without warning, some shooting away, some seeming to merge. Now and then they form a triangle and move together.

About 20 minutes in, the brightest light, the one everyone had taken for a star or a planet, starts drifting their way. It passes roughly a quarter mile from them, heading west to northeast, pulsing a dim blue and then a very bright white, in complete silence. Later a helicopter or plane comes in from the southwest, and soon after that the lights become less frequent and then stop. The report lists five witnesses and about 45 minutes from start to finish.

The witness posted the story to Reddit with a blurry phone photo showing three red lights near a dead tree. Skeptics weighed in fast. One commenter checked the sky for that time and place and said satellites would have been flaring in exactly that spot, and that satellites fading in and out can look like lights changing direction. Another pointed to a spent rocket body passing high in the west-northwest around 9:21 p.m., noted that tumbling rocket stages can pulse slowly, and said the bright light that sat still and then drifted overhead fits a drone better. The family says the number of lights, the brightness and the speed just didn't add up for drones. Two other commenters said they may have seen lights that night too.
