Osceola WI 1952 radar and F-51

CaseOsceola, Wisconsin · Posted Nov 17, 2026

Osceola, Wisconsin. An Air Force radar station near the St. Croix River, 1:30 AM, July 29, 1952.
For one hour the radar painted targets that came out of nowhere, and one of them was clocked at 700 miles per hour.

The men on duty at the radar unit near Osceola watched blips appear suddenly southwest of the station, cross the scope and vanish 30 to 70 nautical miles later. Most came in loose clusters of up to ten, moving 60 to 70 mph on their own headings, some of them turning. One was different: a large, sharply defined target the operators computed at 600 knots, about 700 mph. None of them showed on the height finder.

Then the Ground Observer Corps phoned in a visual report of unidentified objects in the sky, and an F-51 Mustang went up to intercept or identify them. The pilot reported seeing unidentified objects, but he could not close on them or say what they were. The case summary notes some correlation between what the radar painted and what he saw, and photographs of the scope were reviewed by an electronics expert.

The debate: Air Force analysts said anomalous propagation was affecting the radar, and a later note argued the returns were stationary ground clutter flickering under a temperature inversion, with no link to the pilot. But they also admitted "a firm analysis cannot be made" without weather data that was never collected. It happened at the height of the 1952 flap, days after radar tracked unknowns over Washington, DC. Project Blue Book still lists it as Unknown.

If the radar and the pilot both saw something, what was flying over Wisconsin that night?
