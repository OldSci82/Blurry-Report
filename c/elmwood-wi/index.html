Hit By a UFO

CaseElmwood, WI · Posted Nov 25, 2026

A police officer radioed that he was looking at a UFO. Then a blue flash killed his squad car, his headlights and his radio.
Elmwood, Wisconsin. Tuttle Hill above a limestone quarry, about 11:00 PM, April 22, 1976.

Relief officer George Wheeler, a 30 year police veteran and World War II combat pilot, drove up Tuttle Hill thinking an orange glow was a fire. Instead he found a silver craft hovering about 100 feet off the ground, with six bluish white portholes along its side, shadows moving inside, and a top glowing so bright it was "like looking into the sun." He radioed the sheriff's office in Ellsworth. The object shot straight up with a whoosh and a blue flash, and his car went dead.

Dairy farmer David Moots found him dazed, trying to get out of the car. Wheeler told him he had been hit. "Hit by a car?" "No, one of those UFOs." A woman north of town saw a bright orange, moon shaped object on the same hill at 11 o'clock, and a neighboring farmer said his TV cut out a few minutes after 11.

The debate: Wheeler had reported a sighting the year before, and researchers like J. Allen Hynek were wary of repeat witnesses. But investigators Robert Pratt and University of Wisconsin River Falls biology professor Jack Bostrak found other witnesses who backed up the glow. Elmwood never forgot that night.

Would you have driven up that hill?
