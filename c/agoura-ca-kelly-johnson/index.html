Kelly Johnson Agoura 1953 saucer

CaseAgoura, CA · Posted Nov 24, 2026

The man who would design the U-2 and SR-71 spy planes watched a black saucer hang over the Pacific for three minutes, while his own test pilots chased it at 17,000 feet.
Agoura, California. A hilltop ranch three miles west of Agoura, about 5:00 PM, December 16, 1953.

Clarence "Kelly" Johnson, chief engineer at Lockheed, was watching the sunset through his ranch house window when he noticed a dark object above the mountains to the west. He thought it was a smoke trail or a lenticular cloud, but its sharp outline never changed and it did not move for at least three minutes. He sent his wife for the binoculars, ran outside, and watched the long, thin ellipse speed off to the west in a shallow climb. Within 90 seconds it was gone. He placed it roughly over Point Mugu.

At the same time, a Lockheed crew was flying a Navy Super Constellation test flight off the coast. Test pilot Roy Wimmer called out "Look out, there's a flying saucer," and co-pilot Rudy Thoren turned straight toward a dark, sharp edged object that looked like a giant flying wing. They flew at it for about five minutes at some 225 mph and never gained on it. Then it shrank to a speck and vanished, heading west. The next morning, before Thoren could say where or when, Johnson told him.

The debate: Project Blue Book filed it as a lenticular cloud. Johnson wrote that he was "more firmly convinced than ever" such craft exist, and an Air Force general noted he had been reluctant to write the report at all. The Pentagon released the full file on November 20, 2026.

If the best aircraft designer of his era couldn't explain it, who could?
