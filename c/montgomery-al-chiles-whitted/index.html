Montgomery AL Chiles-Whitted wingless craft; Air Force Estimate of the Situation later burned (July 24, 1948)

CaseMontgomery, Alabama · Posted Nov 13, 2026

Two airline pilots said a wingless craft with two rows of glowing windows shot past their DC-3, and the secret Air Force report it inspired was later burned.
Montgomery, Alabama. About 20 miles southwest of the city, 5,000 feet up, 2:45 AM, July 24, 1948.

Captain Clarence S. Chiles and copilot John B. Whitted were flying Eastern Air Lines from Houston to Atlanta under a bright moon, with 20 passengers aboard and 19 of them asleep. Both men had served as officers in the war. Then something came at them.

"It was clear there were no wings present," Chiles wrote in his official statement. "It was powered by some jet or other type of power, shooting flame from the rear some 50 feet. There were two rows of windows... from inside these windows a very bright light was glowing. Underneath the ship there was a blue glow of light." Whitted described a cigar shape about 100 feet long, fatter than a B-29, with large square windows lit from inside. Chiles told reporters they veered left, it veered too, passed about 700 feet off their right side, then pulled up "with a tremendous burst of flame" and zoomed into the clouds. The one passenger awake, Clarence McKelvie of Columbus, Ohio, saw a bright streak flash past his window.

The president of Boeing said it was not one of theirs. The head of the Strategic Air Command said, "The Army hasn't anything like that. I wish we did." According to Edward Ruppelt, later head of Project Blue Book, Air Force intelligence drafted a top secret "Estimate of the Situation" concluding the objects were interplanetary. Chief of Staff Hoyt Vandenberg rejected it, and Ruppelt said copies were sent to the incinerator.

The debate: astronomer J. Allen Hynek decided it was a bright fireball, and that became the official verdict. Skeptics say the "windows" were the airship effect, the mind joining separate lights into one craft. Twenty years later both men, by then Eastern jet captains, still said it was a vehicle, and Whitted added that it did not climb away at all. It simply vanished.

A meteor, or the sighting that convinced Air Force intelligence we had visitors?
