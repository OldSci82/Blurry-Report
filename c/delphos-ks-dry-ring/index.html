The Ring That Stayed Dry

CaseDelphos, KS · Posted Nov 23, 2026

A glowing object shaped like a mushroom hovered over a Kansas sheep farm, and when it shot away it left a glowing ring on the ground that went numb under a woman's fingers.
Delphos, Kansas. The Johnson family farm in Ottawa County, about 7 PM, November 2, 1971.

Sixteen year old Ronald Johnson was doing chores near the sheep pen with the farm dog, Snowball, when he heard a rumble like a washing machine out of balance. About 75 feet away, an object shaped like a toadstool, about 8 feet across, hung 2 to 5 feet above the ground with a shimmering glow running from its base down to the dirt. When it tilted and shot away with a high whine like a jet, Ronald was briefly blinded. His parents, Durel and Erma, ran outside in time to see a light in the sky and a glowing ring on the ground. When Erma touched it, her fingertips went numb.

The next day, reporter Thaddia Smith of the Delphos Republican called the Ottawa County Sheriff. Undersheriff Harlan Enlow found a doughnut shaped ring of bone dry soil in a yard that was otherwise mud, with broken limbs on the trees nearby. They had to force Snowball back to the spot, where he shivered and pawed the ground. Weeks later, investigator Ted Phillips found the soil in the ring still dry about a foot down and the ring outlined in snow that would not melt.

The debate: doubters have blamed a fungus ring or a hoax, but the sheriff's office saw the ring the next day and the family told the same story for the rest of their lives.

What could leave a ring of soil that water would not soak into?
