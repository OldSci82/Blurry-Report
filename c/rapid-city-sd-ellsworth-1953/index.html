Ellsworth AFB radar and jet lock-on (Aug 5, 1953)

CaseRapid City, South Dakota · Posted Nov 26, 2026

A radar on the ground and the radar inside a fighter jet both locked onto the same light over South Dakota.
Rapid City, South Dakota. Ellsworth Air Force Base, the night of August 5, 1953.

Just after 9 PM, a Ground Observer Corps volunteer in Black Hawk, about 8 miles northwest of Rapid City, called Ellsworth about a bright red light low over the hills. It raced off toward Rapid City, turning greener as it picked up speed, then came back. Radar at the base found targets, and airmen sent outside saw a fast light heading south.

An F-84 was vectored in and the pilot made visual contact before the target moved out to 70 miles. A second F-84 climbed to 15,000 feet and watched a light much brighter than a star jump in elevation and change from white to green. His radar gunsight showed a target out ahead, and ground radar tracked it 5 to 10 miles in front of the jet for 80 miles before it went off the scope. The radar scope photos and gun camera film reportedly malfunctioned.

The debate: Project Blue Book never put it on its list of Unknowns, but its own case papers called it unsolved, and Captain Edward Ruppelt featured it in his book as one of the strongest radar and visual cases. Skeptics say bright stars and ground clutter can fool both eyes and radar.

Would you trust a radar lock, two pilots and a ground spotter all at once?
