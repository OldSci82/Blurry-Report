Killian Williamsport Airliner Lights

CaseWilliamsport, PA · Posted Nov 25, 2026

Three glowing objects paced an airliner full of passengers across Pennsylvania for 45 minutes, and at least two other airline crews saw them too.
Williamsport, Pennsylvania. An American Airlines DC-6B from Newark to Detroit, about 8:20 PM, February 24, 1959.

Captain Peter Killian and First Officer James Dee were at 8,500 feet on a clear, moonless night when Killian noticed three bright lights off the left wingtip. He first took them for the belt of Orion, then saw Orion higher overhead. The lights flared "brighter than any star," faded out completely, shifted from yellow orange to a brilliant blue white, and the last one in line kept moving back and forth on its own.

Near Erie, Killian radioed two other American Airlines planes, and both crews found the objects to the south. Over Ohio, a United Airlines crew said three lights paced them for half an hour from near Lock Haven to Youngstown, and ground observers around Akron phoned in nine reports of three UFOs heading west.

The debate: the Air Force said the pilots saw a KC-97 tanker refueling three B-47 bombers near Bradford, Pennsylvania. NICAP pointed out that Bradford is north of the flight path, while every crew saw the lights to the south. Killian never accepted the explanation.

Would you trust three airline crews or the tanker explanation?
