Tuttle OK former 82nd Airborne sergeant sees huge black biped wading the Canadian River (Aug 4, 2025)

CaseTuttle, Oklahoma · Posted Nov 12, 2026

A former 82nd Airborne sergeant looked down from a bridge and saw something huge wading across the river on two legs.
Tuttle, Oklahoma. Highway 4 bridge over the Canadian River, about 15 miles southwest of Oklahoma City, August 4, 2025, 8:15 AM.

Jeff Powers was driving north toward Mustang on his way to work, the same drive he had made every weekday for three years. He always looks up and down the river when he crosses the bridge. That clear morning, the first thing that caught his eye was a wake in the water.

About 80 to 120 yards to the east, a very large black figure was walking across the river on two legs, the water up to about its knees, heading for the north bank. It was completely black, its fur lying flat as if it had just been under water, with a torso he says was wider than a 50 gallon drum. He crossed the bridge, made a full loop and came back to look again. It was gone. Trees grow right to the water's edge there, and one step out of the river would have put it out of sight.

Powers is 62. He served 12 years in the Army, left as a sergeant in the 82nd Airborne, and has hunted deer since he was twelve, so he trusts his distance estimates. He has watched deer from that bridge many times, herds of up to seven. Never anything like this. The BFRO investigator who interviewed him by phone calls him a credible witness.

Would you have kept driving, or turned around like he did?
