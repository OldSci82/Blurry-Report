Florence SC 1952 airport saucer

CaseFlorence, South Carolina · Posted Nov 17, 2026

Airport professionals watched a glowing saucer tilt up and climb out of sight, and the Air Force called it a jet that nobody heard.
Florence, South Carolina. Florence Municipal Airport, just after 5 PM, November 16, 1952.

At dusk on a clear Sunday, the people whose job it was to watch the sky over Florence saw something in it they could not name. The airport manager, a captain with Eastern Air Lines, a weather observer and the field's radio and tower operators, one of them a Civil Aeronautics Administration communicator with 11 years on the job, watched a brilliant object west of the field at roughly 10,000 feet.

It was thick in the center and tapered toward the edges, like a saucer tipped about 30 degrees, and it glowed extremely bright. For about six minutes it moved slowly toward the southwest with no sound and no contrail. A controller watching through binoculars said it tilted up sharply and climbed until it was gone. A driver on the highway between Marion and Sumter reported it too, and phones rang at local newspapers and the CAA. The Air Force file logs sightings at 5:13, 5:15 and 5:21 PM.

The debate: the Air Technical Intelligence Center ruled that a jet had been due over Florence at that time and the object could have been a jet at high altitude. But the witnesses heard nothing and saw no contrail, and Shaw Air Force Base near Sumter reported no jets aloft. Even the record card carries the wrong date, November 17.

Would a whole tower crew really mistake a silent glowing saucer for a jet?
