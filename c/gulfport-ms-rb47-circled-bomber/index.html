RB-47 radar and light, Gulfport MS 1957

CaseGulfport, Mississippi · Posted Nov 18, 2026

An Air Force spy plane picked up a radar signal that ran a full circle around it at 500 mph, and then the pilots saw what was sending it.
Gulfport, Mississippi to Dallas, Texas. An RB-47 out of Forbes Air Force Base, before dawn, July 17, 1957.

A six man RB-47 crew was flying home to Kansas from a training mission over the Gulf of Mexico at 34,500 feet. Just after they crossed the coast near Gulfport, Major Frank McClure, working the electronic gear built to locate enemy radars, picked up a signal that looked exactly like a ground radar. Then it moved. The signal swept up the right side of the plane, crossed in front and slid down the left, running a ring around a bomber doing about 500 mph.

Later in the flight, pilot Major Lewis Chase saw an intensely bright light ahead of them. Ground radar near Dallas, call sign Utah, tracked an object in the same spot, and the plane's own receiver locked onto its signal. When the light blinked out, it vanished from the ground radar and the spy gear at the same moment, then came back on all of them. It stayed with the crew for more than 700 miles and an hour and a half, into Oklahoma.

The debate: Project Blue Book filed the case as an American Airlines flight. Skeptic Philip Klass argued the signals came from ground radars, the first light was a meteor and a later light was an airliner landing at Dallas. Physicist James McDonald, who interviewed the crew, called it one of the strongest cases on record, because one object showed up on eyes, radar and electronics together.

Could an airliner really fool a crew trained to hunt enemy radar?
