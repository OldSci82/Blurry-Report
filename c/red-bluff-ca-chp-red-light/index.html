Red Bluff CA CHP officers Carson and Scott; red-light object that seemed aware of them (August 13, 1960)

CaseRed Bluff, California · Posted Nov 13, 2026

Two California Highway Patrol officers thought an airliner was about to crash in front of them, until it stopped, reversed, and climbed 500 feet.
Red Bluff, California. Tehama County, outside town, 11:50 PM, August 13, 1960.

Officers Charles A. Carson and Stanley Scott were on patrol when a big light dropped toward the road ahead, down to 100 or 200 feet. In their report, it "suddenly reversed completely, at high speed, and gained approximately 500 feet... (and) stopped." A glow surrounded a round or oblong body. "At each end... there were definite red lights. As we watched the object moved again and performed feats that were actually unbelievable."

They tried to close in, but "the object seemed to be aware of us," so they sat still and let it come to them, which it did several times. Each time, a red beam swept the area and lit up the ground, and their radio filled with heavy interference. They radioed the Tehama County Sheriff's office, where deputies also watched it move. A second object joined the first. Both hovered, flashing their beams, then slipped over the eastern horizon. The whole thing lasted about two hours and fifteen minutes. The officers said a local radar base confirmed an unknown that night, then denied it the next day.

The debate: Project Blue Book first said the officers were fooled by Mars and the stars Aldebaran and Betelgeuse, bent by a temperature inversion. When it was pointed out those did not fit the sky at that hour, the answer became Mars and Capella. Physicist James McDonald, who reviewed the case, rejected the explanation.

A planet bent by warm air, or something that knew they were watching?
