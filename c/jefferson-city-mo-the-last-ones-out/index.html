The Last Ones Out

HauntJefferson City, MO · Posted Nov 23, 2026

For 168 years this prison held killers, bank robbers and a future heavyweight champion, and the guides who lead night tours there now say some of the prisoners never left.
Jefferson City, Missouri. The Missouri State Penitentiary on the bluffs of the Missouri River, opened in 1836 and closed in 2004.

When it shut down, it was the oldest operating prison west of the Mississippi. Sonny Liston learned to box inside its walls, Pretty Boy Floyd and Emma Goldman did time there, and in 1967 James Earl Ray escaped hidden in a bread box on a delivery truck, a year before he killed Martin Luther King Jr. A riot in 1954 left four inmates dead, dozens hurt and buildings burned, and from 1938 to 1965 the state put 39 people to death in the small stone gas chamber that still stands on the grounds.

Since the prison opened for tours, guides and guests have described footsteps on empty walkways, cell doors moving on their own, shadow figures in the old 1868 A-Hall, and voices in the underground dungeon cells. Guides tell visitors that blondes and redheads are the ones most likely to be touched, and some guests say it happened to them. Ghost Hunters and Ghost Adventures have both filmed there.

The debate: the site sells ghost tours by the night, so the stories are good for business, and an old stone cellblock full of drafts, echoes and steel doors will always sound like someone is walking right behind you.
