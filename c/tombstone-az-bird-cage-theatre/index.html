The Bird Cage Theatre haunting

HauntTombstone, AZ · Posted Nov 19, 2026

The bullet holes in the walls are real. The 26 people said to have died inside have never been named, and the ghosts keep showing up anyway.
Tombstone, Arizona. The Bird Cage Theatre on Allen Street, opened December 24, 1881, now a museum.

The Bird Cage was a theater, saloon, gambling hall and brothel in a silver boom town, and the legend says it never closed, running day and night for eight years. Curtained boxes hung over the gambling floor, and the New York Times supposedly called it the wildest, wickedest night spot between Basin Street and the Barbary Coast. When the mines flooded and the boom died, it was shut up for decades with much of its furniture still inside.

The ghost stories go back to at least the 1920s, when students at a school across the street said they heard laughter and music from the locked, empty building and smelled cigar smoke. Staff and visitors today describe women in period dresses, men in cowboy hats, unseen hands that push, and a man in black striped pants and a visor who walks across the stage carrying a clipboard. TV ghost hunting crews have called it one of the most haunted buildings in the West.

The debate: the 120 to 140 bullet holes are real, and anyone can count them. The death toll is another story. No source names the 26 dead or dates their deaths, the famous New York Times line has never been matched to an actual issue, and an 1894 newspaper's tale of a twelve dead shootout in the boxes is set a year before the Bird Cage even opened.

Would you stay after closing to count the bullet holes?
