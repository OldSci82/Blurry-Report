Two Frames

CaseMcMinnville, OR · Posted Nov 23, 2026

A farm wife walking home from feeding her rabbits saw a silver disc gliding silently over the field, and her husband grabbed the camera and took two of the most famous UFO photos ever made.
McMinnville, Oregon. The Trent farm near Sheridan, about 13 miles southwest of McMinnville, about 7:30 PM, May 11, 1950.

Evelyn Trent called to her husband Paul, who ran for the family's loaded Kodak and snapped two pictures of the slow, metallic, disc shaped object before it sped off to the west. Paul's father said he saw it too. The Trents later said they thought it might be a secret military plane and worried the pictures would bring trouble, so they did not rush to show anyone. A local banker put the prints in his window, the McMinnville Telephone-Register ran them on the front page on June 8, and Life magazine printed them weeks later.

In 1967, astronomer William K. Hartmann of the Air Force funded Condon Committee studied the negatives and interviewed the Trents. He wrote that this was one of the few UFO reports where everything he checked appeared consistent with an extraordinary, silvery, metallic, disc shaped flying object, tens of meters across. He also admitted it could be a small model hanging from a wire.

The debate: skeptics Philip Klass and Robert Sheaffer called it a hoax, pointing to shadows that look like morning rather than evening and a shape that resembles an old car mirror. In 2013 a French research group said it could see a thread above the object. Evelyn and Paul Trent insisted it was real until they died in the 1990s, and McMinnville now throws one of the biggest UFO festivals in the country every spring.

Do those two photos show a real craft or a model on a string?
