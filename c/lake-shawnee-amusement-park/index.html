Lake Shawnee Amusement Park (Princeton, WV)

HauntPrinceton, West Virginia · Posted Nov 27, 2026

People say the rusted swings at this abandoned park still move when there is no wind.
Princeton, West Virginia. Lake Shawnee Amusement Park, Mercer County.

In 1783 this land belonged to settler Mitchell Clay. While he was away, three of his children were attacked here. A daughter and a son were killed, and another son was taken captive. In 1926 Conley Snidow opened an amusement park on the same ground, with a Ferris wheel, a swing ride, a pool and cabins for coal mining families.

Two people drowned in the lake during those years, and in 1966 a young girl was killed on the swing ride. The park closed in 1967, reopened briefly in 1987 and shut for good in 1988. In the early 1990s, work on the property turned up Native American artifacts, and Marshall University archaeologists uncovered thirteen skeletons, mostly elderly people and young children.

The debate: the owners later began running Halloween paranormal tours, and the park has been on Scariest Places on Earth, Ghost Lab and The Dead Files. Skeptics say a few sad accidents over 62 years are not a curse, and some call the tours a business built on graves. Visitors still describe a little girl in a pink dress near the swings.

Would you walk past those swings after dark?
