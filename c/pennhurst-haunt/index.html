Pennhurst State School and Hospital

HauntSpring City, Pennsylvania · Posted Oct 30, 2026

Spring City, Pennsylvania. Pennhurst State School and Hospital.

Built on Crab Hill in Chester County in the early 1900s, Pennhurst was a state institution for people with intellectual and physical disabilities, its red brick buildings linked by tunnels with walkways on top. In 1968, WCAU-TV reporter Bill Baldini aired a five-part exposé of conditions inside. In 1981, Time described a place with "a history of being understaffed, dirty and violent," and in 1983 nine employees were indicted on charges ranging from slapping and beating patients to arranging for patients to assault each other.

The case that ended it started with Terri Lee Halderman, a resident who came home with unexplained bruises. In 1977, a federal judge ruled the conditions violated residents' constitutional rights, and Pennhurst closed on December 9, 1987, after 79 years.

Then came the controversy. By 2010 the administration building had reopened as the Pennhurst Asylum seasonal haunted attraction, successful but controversial among locals and people once tied to Pennhurst. Overnight paranormal investigations began in 2017, and A&E filmed World's Biggest Ghost Hunt: Pennhurst Asylum there in 2019. Ghost hunters say something still walks those halls. Critics say the real horror is what happened to the people who lived there.
