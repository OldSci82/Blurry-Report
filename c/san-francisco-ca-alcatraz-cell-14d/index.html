Alcatraz Cell 14D haunt

HauntSan Francisco, CA · Posted Nov 12, 2026

They say one cell on Alcatraz never warms up, no matter the season.
San Francisco, California. Alcatraz Island, D Block, cell 14D.

The cellhouse went up between 1910 and 1912, when Alcatraz was still a military prison. As a federal penitentiary from 1934 to 1963 it held men like Al Capone, and D Block held the most troublesome of them in solitary. Six cells at the end were known as the Hole, dark isolation cells where prisoners were sent for punishment.

That is where the stories gather. Cell 14D is said to stay icy cold. Staff and visitors tell of screams, clanging metal, cell doors that move on their own, and faint banjo music drifting through the empty cellhouse, an instrument Capone really did play while he was locked up there. The grimmest version has an inmate screaming all night about a creature with glowing eyes, then found dead in the morning.

The debate: the National Park Service, which runs the island, does not tell any of these stories, and no prison record backs up the glowing eyes death. Fact checkers trace the tales to local legend collections and ghost tour lore. Meanwhile Alcatraz has a new fight on its hands. In 2025 President Trump proposed reopening it as a prison, and in April the White House asked for 152 million dollars to rebuild it. The Bureau of Prisons is still studying the idea, and critics question pouring that much into a salt damaged island. Right now it is closed to visitors until November 18 for dock repairs, so the only ones walking D Block tonight are the ones in the stories.

Would you spend one night locked in cell 14D?
