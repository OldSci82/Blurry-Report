Point Lookout Lighthouse haunt

HauntScotland, MD · Posted Nov 13, 2026

Maryland's most haunted lighthouse stands beside the ground where nearly 4,000 prisoners of war died.
Scotland, Maryland. Point Lookout Lighthouse, Point Lookout State Park, where the Potomac River meets the Chesapeake Bay.

The light was first lit in 1830. When its first keeper died that December, his daughter Ann Davis took over and kept it until 1847. Then came the Civil War. After Gettysburg, the Union opened Camp Hoffman on 40 acres behind a 15 foot stockade. It was built for 10,000 men and held 20,000 or more. About 50,000 Confederate prisoners passed through, living in crowded tents through bay winters with bad water, and nearly 4,000 died, mostly of disease. They lie in a mass grave under a monument bearing their names.

The stories took hold in the 1970s, when park staff lived in the keeper's house. Resident Laura Berg described heavy, booted footsteps in the hallway and a smell of decay in the guest room in the winter of 1979 and 1980. In January 1980 parapsychologist Hans Holzer brought in a team of psychics, and a seance followed that February. A photograph from that night is said to show a Confederate soldier. Volunteers have described a crowd of men's voices in an empty living room, a nailed shut basement door whose knob rattled by itself, and the clatter of tin plates and galloping horses outside the gate.

The debate: park superintendent Gerald Sword, who lived there himself, noted there was no evidence any prisoner was ever held in the house. Then the Navy fenced the building off and left it empty for a quarter century, which only fed the legend. Maryland got it back in 2006 and opened it as a museum in May 2025. Historians add that ghost tales can crowd out the camp's full story, including the many United States Colored Troops who stood guard there, and the Confederate memorial park nearby keeps Point Lookout in the middle of an argument over how the war is remembered.

Would you walk the shoreline at Point Lookout after dark?
