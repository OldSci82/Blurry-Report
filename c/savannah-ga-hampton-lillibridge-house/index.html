Hampton-Lillibridge House haunt

HauntSavannah, GA · Posted Nov 11, 2026

A bishop performed an exorcism on this Savannah house in 1963. Within a week, the footsteps were back.
Savannah, Georgia. The Hampton-Lillibridge House, 507 East St. Julian Street, near Washington Square.

Rhode Island native Hampton Lillibridge built this steep roofed house with a rooftop widow's walk in the 1790s, and it looks like it wandered down from a New England seaport. It survived the great fire of 1820 and spent years as a boarding house, where legend says a young sailor took his own life in an upstairs bedroom.

Then came Jim Williams, the preservationist and antiques dealer later at the center of Midnight in the Garden of Good and Evil. He bought the run down house and had it moved from East Bryan Street to St. Julian Street. During the move, part of an adjoining building collapsed and a workman was killed. Workers restoring it reported voices and footsteps in the empty house. Williams and friends chased the sounds floor by floor up to the widow's walk, and then heard them below. One friend who went upstairs alone was found face down, soaked in sweat, saying an icy force was pulling him toward an open chimney shaft about thirty feet deep. Neighbors described a woman screaming and a man in a black suit and bow tie standing in an upstairs window.

On December 7, 1963, the Episcopal bishop Albert Rhett Stewart performed a 45 minute exorcism. Within a week, the story goes, it all came back worse. Workers had also found an old tabby crypt, half filled with water, under the site. They told Williams it was empty and covered it back up.

The debate: even the dates do not agree. Tour accounts put the purchase and exorcism in 1963, while the house's encyclopedia entry says Williams bought it in 1969. Larger death tolls from the move get repeated, but the documented accounts point to one fatal accident. It is a private home today, and much of the legend lives on in Savannah's busy ghost tour trade. Still, neighbors and owners have been telling the same stories for sixty years.

Would you stand outside it after dark?
