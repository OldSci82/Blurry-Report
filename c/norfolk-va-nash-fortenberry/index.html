Nash-Fortenberry 1952 discs

CaseNorfolk, VA · Posted Nov 23, 2026

Two airline pilots watched eight glowing red discs race in under their plane and flip around in a hairpin turn "like balls bouncing off a wall."
Norfolk, Virginia. Over the Chesapeake Bay, 20 to 25 miles north of Norfolk, 9:12 PM, July 14, 1952.

Pan American First Officer William B. Nash and Second Officer William H. Fortenberry were flying a DC-4 airliner at 8,000 feet on a clear night when they saw a red glow on the ground ahead, near Newport News. It turned into six round, flat objects shaped like coins, each about 100 feet across, glowing a fiery red and flying in a tight line about 2,000 feet above the bay. Almost directly under the airliner, all six stopped short and reversed direction in a sharp 150 degree turn. Two more, brighter than the rest, came up from behind the plane and joined them. Then all eight blinked out together, lit back up, and climbed as a group toward Newport News before winking out one by one.

The debate: ground witnesses reported similar lights that night, the Air Force questioned both pilots, and Project Blue Book filed the case as an unknown. Later skeptics blamed ground lights seen through haze, fireflies trapped between the cockpit window panes, and even a mirage of the planet Venus.

Could two veteran airline pilots really mistake fireflies for eight discs?
